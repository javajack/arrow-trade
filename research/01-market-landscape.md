# Indian Retail Broking Market Landscape

Research date: 11 June 2026. Primary wedge assumed: active F&O/derivatives traders and pro/API-first users; secondary expansion: long-term investors.

## Executive Takeaways

India's retail broking market has moved from pure account-acquisition growth to regulation-constrained monetisation. The May 2026 NSE active-client base is still large at 4.56 crore, but below March 2025's 4.92 crore and essentially flat versus March 2026's 4.57 crore, using NSE Report 1C files for FY27, FY26 and FY25 ([FY27 May 2026 XLS](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/miscellaneous/ArbitrationReports/isc_report1C_2627.xls), [FY26 XLS](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/miscellaneous/ArbitrationReports/isc_report1C_2526.xls), [FY25 XLS](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/miscellaneous/ArbitrationReports/isc_report1C_2425.xls)).

The top end is concentrated but not settled. Groww leads by a wide margin and is still adding clients; Zerodha and Angel One remain the monetisation benchmarks but are shrinking YoY; ICICI, SBI Securities and Dhan are the most important share gainers among the top 10. Upstox has lost the most ground.

The core profit pool is still active trading, especially options, but 2024-25 SEBI changes make the old F&O flywheel less reliable: upfront option premium collection, larger lot sizes, fewer weekly expiries, expiry-day margin tightening and intraday position monitoring were introduced by SEBI's 1 October 2024 circular ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2024/measures-to-strengthen-equity-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_87208.html)). SEBI's July 2024 consultation paper documented the policy rationale: FY24 individual index-derivatives traders had Rs 51,689 crore of net trading losses before transaction costs, and low expiry-day option premiums acted like a cheap speculative lottery ticket ([SEBI consultation paper](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/jul-2024/consultation-paper-on-measures-to-strengthen-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_85279.html)).

Whitespace exists, but not in "another cheap brokerage app." The most attractive wedge is a professional trading stack that combines reliability, transparent cost/risk telemetry, API/algo tooling, strategy analytics, tax/P&L intelligence, and margin/MTF/pledge workflows. For long-term investors, the gap is a clean unified wealth layer that does not feel like an upsell farm.

### Why Winners Are Winning

**Groww: simple investor-first acquisition, then broadening.** Groww's wedge was mutual funds and first-time investing, not professional trading. That created a large low-friction funnel, and the stock/F&O product rode that distribution. Chittorgarh's 2026 broker report states Groww is the top broker and is followed by Zerodha, Angel One and ICICIdirect, and says its broker rankings are updated monthly from exchange data ([Chittorgarh broker ranking page](https://www.chittorgarh.com/report/top_20_share_brokers_in_india_by_clients_at_nse/1/)). Groww's strategic advantage is UX simplicity, brand trust among new investors, and cross-sell from MF/SIP into stocks. The weakness is that its primary persona is still less differentiated for serious F&O users.

**ICICI and SBICAP: captive bank ecosystems and trust.** Both gained in FY26 while many discount brokers lost clients. The reason is not superior pro-trading UI; it is distribution, bank trust, salary/banking relationships, branch/RM comfort for older investors, and integrated pledging/funds flows. ICICI overtook Upstox to become fourth largest by active clients in FY26, according to ET's NSE-data summary ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/market-swings-trigger-retail-rethink-active-client-base-of-top-brokers-falls/articleshow/130295722.cms?from=mdr)).

**Dhan / Raise: focused pro-trader positioning.** Dhan is the clearest proof that a narrower active-trader wedge can still gain share against incumbents. Its growth is driven by TradingView-style charting, options-focused UX, API/algo positioning, web-first execution, and trader-community credibility. It moved ahead of Motilal Oswal in FY26 rankings per ET's NSE-data summary ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/market-swings-trigger-retail-rethink-active-client-base-of-top-brokers-falls/articleshow/130295722.cms?from=mdr)). This is the most relevant strategic benchmark for a new entrant.

**Paytm Money: recovery from a small-investor base.** Paytm Money's FY26 active-client YoY is positive from a lower March 2025 base, but May 2026 momentum is negative. The brand/funnel helps, but it does not yet read as the destination for pro derivatives traders.

### Why Losers Are Shrinking

**Upstox: acquisition-led growth without durable engagement.** Upstox is the steepest top-10 decliner: -27.6% in FY26 and another -3.2% through May 2026. The likely causes are weaker retention after paid/referral-led account acquisition, less visible product differentiation versus Zerodha/Dhan for serious traders, and a customer base more sensitive to market volatility and regulatory tightening.

**Zerodha: excellent product, but mature base and exposed F&O revenue mix.** Zerodha remains the strongest quality benchmark: Kite, Console, Coin, Varsity, clean pricing, ecosystem credibility, and bootstrapped trust. But it lost 12.6% active clients in FY26 and is down again by May 2026. Its own leadership has warned of regulatory pressure: press coverage of Nithin Kamath's 2025 anniversary post reported a 40% YoY decline in Q2 FY2025 brokerage revenue and the possibility of charging for equity delivery if pressures persist ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/ceo-nithin-kamath-warns-zerodha-may-start-charging-millions-of-its-users-if-/articleshow/124252402.cms)).

**Angel One: high active-trader scale, hit by F&O slowdown and client-quality reset.** Angel has the best public-market operating disclosure among scaled discount/full-service hybrids, and its franchise is still large. But its active-client count fell 10.8% in FY26 and slightly further by May 2026. It is exposed to the same options-volume cycle, plus a broader affiliate/advisor acquisition model that can create lower-quality active clients.

**Traditional full-service brokers: strong trust but weak modern UX.** Kotak, HDFC, Motilal, Sharekhan, IIFL, 5paisa, Mirae and PhonePe all declined in FY26 or May 2026. For banks/full-service brokers, the persistent gap is pro-grade product velocity. For discount brokers with weaker differentiation, the issue is that "Rs 20/trade" is no longer enough.

## Revenue Models and Unit Economics

### Core Revenue Lines

| Revenue line | How it works | Why it matters now |
|---|---|---|
| Brokerage | Flat fee, typically up to Rs 20/order for intraday and derivatives; often zero for equity delivery at discount brokers. | The headline revenue pool is volume-driven. Regulatory limits on option churn directly hit this line. |
| Exchange transaction-charge spread, historical | Exchanges charged slabs by broker volume; brokers could charge clients a standard rate and retain the slab benefit. | SEBI's "true-to-label" / uniform charge push reduced this spread, forcing brokers to pass actual charges and making brokerage revenue cleaner but lower. |
| Float / interest income | Client funds and settlement balances can create treasury income within regulatory constraints; brokers also earn interest on margin funding and collateral workflows. | Interest income becomes more important as brokerage compresses, but client-fund handling is tightly regulated. |
| MTF / margin funding | Broker/NBFC funds delivery positions against collateral, earning interest. | One of the biggest diversification lines because it monetises investors, not only traders. Risk is credit, concentration and market drawdown. |
| Pledge / collateral / DP charges | Pledge, unpledge, sell-side DP, demat and transaction fees. | Often invisible to users; transparency can be a trust wedge. |
| Distribution | Mutual funds, bonds, loans, insurance, smallcases/wealth products, IPO financing/referrals. | Useful for ARPU diversification, but can contaminate user trust if pushed aggressively. |
| API / pro tooling subscriptions | Paid APIs, data, advanced analytics, low-latency tooling, strategy logs. | Under-monetised in India relative to serious trader willingness to pay for reliability and automation. |

### F&O Dependence

The discount-broking engine is structurally tied to F&O because equity delivery is free or cheap, while active options traders create repeated chargeable orders. SEBI's study update reported that 91% of individual equity-derivatives traders lost money in FY25, and that individual EDS turnover in premium terms fell 11% YoY for Dec 2024-May 2025 after the October 2024 measures ([Economic Times coverage of SEBI study](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/91-of-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy25-despite-sebi-curbs-study/articleshow/122302956.cms)).

For a new entrant, the lesson is uncomfortable: the most profitable retail users are also the most regulator-sensitive and outcome-sensitive. A sustainable product should help serious traders trade better, size better, automate safely, and measure true costs, not merely increase churn.

### Unit-Economic Implications

- CAC has likely risen for undifferentiated brokers because account opening is commoditised and active-client conversion is falling.
- Payback depends on activation into F&O, MTF, or distribution. A pure long-term-investor account has low brokerage ARPU unless it converts into MTF/wealth products.
- Regulatory changes shift economics from hidden spread to explicit fees. This favours trusted, transparent brands and hurts brokers reliant on opaque pass-throughs.
- Reliability incidents are expensive: active traders churn quickly after order placement, login, margin, feed or expiry-day failures.
- API/pro users can justify subscription-like revenue if uptime, order semantics, logs, simulation, risk controls and support are credible.

## Regulatory Backdrop

### SEBI Equity Index Derivatives Measures

SEBI's 1 October 2024 circular introduced the following key changes ([SEBI circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/oct-2024/measures-to-strengthen-equity-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_87208.html)):

- Upfront collection of net options premium payable by option buyers, included in intraday margin snapshots.
- Removal of calendar-spread treatment on expiry day for contracts expiring that day.
- Intraday monitoring of index-derivatives position limits with at least four snapshots during the day.
- Minimum index-derivatives contract value recalibrated upward to Rs 15 lakh at introduction/review, with Rs 15-20 lakh band.
- Weekly index derivatives rationalised: each exchange may offer weekly expiry only on one benchmark index.
- Extra tail-risk coverage on options-expiry day.

These changes attacked the specific behaviours SEBI highlighted in the consultation paper: expiry-day hyperactivity, very short holding periods, low-premium far-OTM speculation, and the concentration of retail losses ([SEBI consultation paper](https://www.sebi.gov.in/reports-and-statistics/reports/jul-2024/consultation-paper-on-measures-to-strengthen-index-derivatives-framework-for-increased-investor-protection-and-market-stability_85279.html)).

### Impact on Broker Revenue

The visible effects are:

- Active-client decline: NSE active clients fell from 4.92 crore in March 2025 to 4.57 crore in March 2026 and 4.56 crore in May 2026.
- Derivatives turnover pressure: SEBI's post-curb study, as reported by ET, found Dec 2024-May 2025 index-options turnover down 9% in premium terms and 29% in notional terms YoY, while unique individual EDS traders fell 20% YoY ([Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/91-of-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-in-fy25-despite-sebi-curbs-study/articleshow/122302956.cms)).
- Broker strategy shifts: monetise equity delivery, push MTF, wealth/distribution, paid pro tooling, advisory-like education without crossing regulatory lines, and institutional/API products.

### True-to-Label / Uniform Charges

SEBI's true-to-label direction is a structural change even where details vary by exchange and segment: brokers must not show one charge label to clients while retaining exchange slab benefits invisibly. The practical result is lower exchange-charge spread income and greater need to price explicitly. Broker commentary in 2025 tied revenue pressure to this package of regulatory changes, alongside F&O curbs and margin rules ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/ceo-nithin-kamath-warns-zerodha-may-start-charging-millions-of-its-users-if-/articleshow/124252402.cms)).

Strategically, this favours a new entrant willing to be radically transparent:

- Real-time all-in breakeven per order, including brokerage, exchange, STT, stamp, GST, SEBI fees, DP and slippage estimate.
- Historical cost attribution by strategy, instrument, expiry, broker fee line and tax bucket.
- No teaser pricing that later monetises through hidden DP or platform charges.

## Whitespace for a New Entrant

### 1. Pro Trader Operating System

Build for a serious F&O user who already knows Zerodha, Dhan and TradingView.

Must-have gaps to exploit:

- Strategy-level P&L: tag every order to a strategy, expiry, thesis, setup and rule; show net P&L after all charges and taxes.
- Options risk console: Greeks, payoff, probability, margin impact, breakeven drift, IV percentile and event risk in one place.
- Expiry-day guardrails: position limits, premium-at-risk, auto square-off rules, circuit/liquidity checks and "can I exit?" depth warnings.
- Post-trade journal: automatic screenshots, order replay, error taxonomy, slippage attribution and rule-violation detection.
- Multi-account/family/pro desk mode: not institutional prime brokerage, but structured enough for serious retail desks.

### 2. API-First Brokerage

Current APIs are useful but not productised as a complete developer platform.

Exploit:

- Deterministic order-state machine with idempotency keys and audit logs.
- Sandbox with realistic fills, rejected orders, RMS behaviour and exchange holidays.
- Paper trading tied to live data and then one-click promotion to live.
- Versioned SDKs, webhooks, replayable market data, latency dashboards and incident status pages.
- Strategy risk limits enforced server-side, not only in the user's bot.

### 3. Transparent Cost and Risk Layer

Most brokers show brokerage calculators as static utilities. A new platform can make true cost a live part of trading.

Exploit:

- Per-order and per-strategy all-in cost before order placement.
- Break-even move and required win-rate by strategy.
- Monthly "where your money went" ledger: brokerage, exchange, STT, GST, stamp, DP, slippage, funding interest.
- Compare "trade less / size differently / use different expiry" scenarios.

### 4. MTF and Collateral Done Well

MTF is a major monetisation path, but user experience is often opaque.

Exploit:

- Plain-English funding cost, liquidation path, haircut, pledge status and interest accrual.
- Portfolio-level leverage health with stress scenarios.
- Transparent pledge/unpledge timelines and failure states.
- Conservative risk controls as brand positioning, not friction.

### 5. Long-Term Investor Expansion

Once active-trader credibility is established, expand without diluting the wedge.

Exploit:

- Direct equity + MF + ETF + bonds + SGB + fixed income in one tax-aware portfolio.
- Tax harvesting, capital-gains forecasting and ITR-ready exports.
- Family portfolio, nominees, inheritance checklist, document vault.
- Research workflows: watchlists, thesis notes, alerts, earnings calendar, corporate-action explainer.

## Strategic Positioning Recommendation

Do not enter as "cheaper Zerodha." Enter as "the professional trading and automation broker that makes every risk, cost and system state visible."

The product promise should be:

- More reliable than the discount-broker baseline.
- More transparent than bank brokers.
- More complete than standalone charting/API tools.
- More risk-aware than F&O gamification apps.

The first wedge should be active traders and API users because they have urgent switching pain and higher ARPU. But the brand must avoid looking like it profits from churn. The defensible angle is "trade with institutional-grade controls at retail scale."
