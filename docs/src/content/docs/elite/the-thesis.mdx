## The market gap, stated precisely

The Indian algo market sells four cheap, commoditized things — and none of them is what a big-ticket trader actually needs:

| What the market sells today | Public price floor | What it still leaves the client to do |
|---|---|---|
| Broker APIs (Kite Connect, DhanHQ, Fyers) | Free–₹500/mo ([pricing research](/elite/pricing-and-economics/)) | Build, host, register, monitor and risk-manage their own strategies |
| No-code/strategy platforms (Tradetron, AlgoTest, Streak) | ₹0–₹9,000/mo | Pick/operate strategies on third-party rails; manage static IP, VPS, slippage |
| Options analytics (Sensibull, Quantsapp) | Subscription | Decide and execute everything themselves |
| Discount brokerage | ₹20/order | Get no service, no governance, no institutional capability |

A trader with ₹50 lakh or ₹5 crore can buy all of these and *still* has no access to a vetted, capacity-managed, professionally-operated, exchange-registered strategy catalogue running in their own account with a human desk behind it. That is the gap — and it is exactly the capability an HFT firm has and a SaaS vendor does not.

## The thesis: sell the scarce regulated bundle, never the commodity

Arrow Elite must refuse to compete as "another algo platform." Its price and defensibility come from the bundle that is **scarce because it is hard and regulated**, not from being cheaper or having more features:

Each element of the bundle is independently hard to assemble and, combined, has **no public like-for-like competitor in India** ([competitive research](/elite/pricing-and-economics/)). The [regulatory page](/elite/regulatory-and-trust/) shows why: it takes an entity that is simultaneously a broker *and* a Research Analyst *and* an HFT operator to ship it legally — a combination iRage uniquely has.

## Positioning: institutional capability, retail-accessible, honestly framed

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| Category | A governed **broker-hosted strategy facility** for serious capital — not a marketplace, not PMS, not a "returns" product |
| Promise | Access to institutional-grade automation and execution you cannot build alone, run in your own account, with the system state visible and the risk capped |
| Enemy | The fragility of DIY algos, the registration bureaucracy, opaque capacity, fragmented tooling, and discount-broker indifference at crore scale |
| Tone | Precise, institutional, conflict-honest; no hype, no return claims, no "HFT alpha for retail" overreach |
| Proof objects | iRage's verified registrations, capacity ledgers, latency/order-audit, conflict disclosures, named desk, CA-ready reporting |

The brand line — *"broker-registered automated strategies in your own account, no returns promised or represented"* — is doing double duty: it is both the pitch and the compliance posture. This is the [trust-by-proof philosophy](/moats/competing-moats/) from docs1, escalated for an audience that does professional diligence.

## How Elite dissolves docs1's LTV paradox

[docs1's economics page](/viability/economics-ltv-paradox/) confronted an uncomfortable truth: the mass F&O wedge is people who lose money (91% per SEBI) and quit — a churning, self-liquidating base. Arrow Elite is the structural answer, not a patch:

| docs1 mass-retail problem | Arrow Elite inversion |
|---|---|
| Thousands of low-ARPU users who churn when they lose | Hundreds of high-ARPU clients who stay for capability and service |
| Revenue = trade frequency of losers | Revenue = subscription + brokerage + capacity fees from sophisticated capital |
| CAC inflation in a saturated demat market | Targeted, signal-led, concierge acquisition of identified whales |
| Trust cold-start at small scale | Trust anchored by iRage's institutional pedigree |

This is a **low-volume, high-ARPU** business — the opposite shape from mass retail, and a far healthier one. A few hundred ₹1cr+ relationships paying for governed capability beats a million ₹10k accounts churning on losses. Elite is not Arrow's charity tier; it is plausibly its best margin.

<Aside type="caution" title="The honesty constraint that makes it work">
The thesis only holds if Arrow never crosses into the things that would make it a marketplace, a PMS, or a returns product. The moment it ranks strategies by performance or implies expected profit, it loses both the legal footing and the institutional credibility. The discipline *is* the strategy — see the [Pre-Mortem](/elite/pre-mortem/).
</Aside>
