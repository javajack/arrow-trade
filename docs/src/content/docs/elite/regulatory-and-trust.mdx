<Aside type="tip" title="Why the RA registration is the keystone">
A broker distributing *decision-making* strategies whose logic the client can't see is, by SEBI's definition, distributing **black-box algos** — which legally requires the provider to be a **registered Research Analyst** with a research report per algo. iRage already holds RA registration **INH000025425**. Most brokers and every SaaS vendor do not. This single fact is what makes a 100+ black-box strategy catalogue buildable by Arrow and effectively no one else.

## The zero-client-registration mechanic (validated)

The product's headline promise — clients run institutional algos without filing anything with SEBI — is legally sound under the [Feb 2025 framework](/algo/regulatory-framework/), with conditions:

| Algo source | Client files own registration? | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Client self-developed, ≤10 OPS | No | Static IP/API/2FA/broker controls still apply |
| Client self-developed, >10 OPS | **Yes** (through broker) | Auditor cert, write-ups, T+10 |
| **Broker-generated (Arrow's catalogue)** | **No** | Broker obtains exchange permission per algo, tags orders with the exchange algo ID, remains principal |
| Empanelled vendor algo | No | Vendor empanelled; broker due-diligences |

So Arrow's catalogue is the broker-generated path: **Arrow registers each strategy once; eligible clients subscribe with no filing of their own.** The obligations Arrow cannot escape: exchange permission for *each* algo, order tagging, change-control, RMS, surveillance, grievance handling, and being the **principal** (it can never tell a client "that's between you and the vendor").

## Black-box obligations and change control

| Obligation | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Provider must be SEBI RA | iRage's INH000025425 — monitor renewal/status, show in disclosures |
| Research report per black-box algo | An RA-controlled dossier per strategy: objective, universe, signal families, risk controls, assumptions, stress behaviour, capacity, conflicts |
| Confirm report to exchange | Filing-checklist item for every black-box registration |
| **Fresh registration on logic change** | Signal/model/entry-exit/hedge/alpha changes = new algo + new dossier. Client risk-cap changes (max loss, allowed instruments) are parameters, not logic — but compliance decides the line |

This change-control reality is *why* the catalogue is an ops commitment ([Algo Cloud page](/elite/algo-cloud/)): iterating a strategy is not a code push, it is a re-filing.

## The return-claims ban shapes everything client-facing

SEBI's **Sep 2, 2022 circular** (via [NSE/COMP/53555](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/COMP53555.pdf)) bars brokers providing algo services from referring — directly or indirectly — to past or expected returns/performance, and from associating with platforms that do. Practically:

- No CAGR, monthly return, "expected profit", backtested-return headline, win-rate, or creator leaderboard ranked by returns.
- If Arrow ever lists third-party strategies, their landing pages, screenshots and videos must also be compliant.
- Quality is shown through **risk controls, capacity state, operational metrics, and the client's own post-trade reporting** — never performance marketing ([compliant display detail](/elite/algo-cloud/)).

## Suitability & accredited-investor gating

SEBI's accredited-investor framework (Aug 2021) is a useful *additional* eligibility filter for high-risk strategies — but it removes **none** of the algo-framework duties (RA, registration, return-ban, broker-principal, static IP, RMS). And it does **not** authorize PMS/AIF-style discretion, which is out of scope. Suitability controls SEBI would expect:

- Product-governance committee approving each strategy before filing (target client, risk class, leverage, drawdown, capacity, kill criteria, conflicts).
- Client segmentation: minimum net worth/capital, derivatives activation, experience, loss-bearing capacity, accredited status where applicable.
- Explicit F&O risk acceptance, per-client hard risk caps, cooling-off on first activation, no guaranteed-return arrangements.

## The conflict that must be governed: broker × HFT prop

Arrow Elite's differentiator — iRage's prop/HFT pedigree — is also its sharpest risk. The same group runs proprietary HFT/market-making *and* distributes strategies to clients. SEBI (post the NSE co-location history) will scrutinize this hard, and it must be governed, not disclaimed.

| Conflict | Governance answer |
|---|---|
| Prop could see client order flow / front-run | Hard information barriers; no client order/position data to prop researchers; access logs; trade-ahead surveillance |
| Prop competes with client strategies for queue/liquidity | Capacity caps; documented client-vs-prop routing fairness; queue-priority surveillance |
| Marketing implies clients get prop alpha | Ban prop-performance references; sell controls and risk, never prop P&L |
| Market-maker obligations vs client interest | Avoid distributing client strategies in instruments where iRage has designated MM obligations unless specifically approved and disclosed |
| Same logic in prop and client products | Separate signals/venues/instruments/latency classes/capacity pools — or disclose and govern explicitly |

<Aside type="caution" title="The trust equation">
At crore scale, trust is binary and catastrophic-tailed — and an HFT-owned broker starts with a *harder* question than a normal one: "is my broker trading against me?" The answer must be a visible governance package (information barriers, no-prop-priority guarantee, conflict disclosures, board oversight, surveillance), plus the [docs1 trust machinery](/viability/trust-cold-start/) — named desk, withdrawal SLA, ledger reconciliation, custody proof. iRage's clean RA/broker complaint record and institutional standing are the cold-start anchor; weak conflict governance would forfeit it instantly. This is the headline item in the [Pre-Mortem](/elite/pre-mortem/).
</Aside>
