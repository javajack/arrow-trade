The product is a **broker-hosted strategy catalogue**: Arrow/iRage builds and pre-registers a library of strategies with the exchanges; an eligible client browses, subscribes, sets risk caps, and the strategy executes **in the client's own Arrow account**. The client never files a SEBI strategy registration, never runs a VPS, never manages a static IP. That turnkey experience is the entire value — and it is only legal because of iRage's broker + RA registrations ([regulatory page](/elite/regulatory-and-trust/)).

Under the SEBI framework, a **broker-generated, exchange-registered algo** can be used by clients without their own strategy registration — the client-side registration burden only applies to *self-developed* algos above the 10-OPS threshold ([validated in detail](/elite/regulatory-and-trust/)). Arrow does the filing, the RA dossier, the change-control and the capacity management; the client gets institutional automation as a subscription.

<Aside type="caution" title="'100+ strategies' is an operations commitment, not a toggle">
Each strategy needs its own exchange permission, a black-box RA research report, version control, and **fresh registration on any logic change**. A catalogue of 100+ is therefore a standing compliance-operations function, not a product menu. The number is a promise about Arrow's ops capacity as much as about client choice — budget for it.
</Aside>

## What the catalogue contains (categorized by behaviour, never by returns)

| Category | Examples | Box type | Who it serves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Execution algos** | TWAP/VWAP, participation, order slicing, hedged execution | White-box (logic disclosed) | Anyone trading size; easiest to register/explain |
| **Medium-frequency systematic** | Seconds-to-minutes options/stat-arb variants, capacity-capped | Black-box (RA-governed) | Pros, HNI, UHNI allocations |
| **Risk overlays** | Auto-hedging, delta/gamma management, stop orchestration, expiry guardrails | Mixed | Option sellers, family offices |
| **Discretionary power tools** | One-tap/chart trading, trailing SL, basket/iceberg, emergency flatten | White-box | HNI discretionary traders, supertraders |

Crucially, the catalogue is organized by **instrument, holding period, automation type and risk-control profile** — never by performance, because [SEBI bans return/ranking claims](/elite/regulatory-and-trust/). There is no leaderboard. There is no "top strategy." There is a governed menu with honest risk and capacity labels.

<Aside type="note" title="Why iRage would share strategies at all (the alpha-decay honesty)">
iRage will not hand clients its core latency-arbitrage edge — that decays the instant it is crowded, and a rational firm keeps it prop. What is genuinely shareable is the *ladder of strategies whose edge does not depend on nanosecond exclusivity*: execution algos, medium-frequency systematic variants, risk overlays, and decayed-but-still-useful prop research run with honest capacity caps. The honest claim is **"iRage's experience in automated markets informs a broker-registered, capacity-capped catalogue"** — not "we give you the prop desk's alpha." Stating this plainly is what keeps the product credible to sophisticated clients who know how capacity works.
</Aside>

## Capacity discipline (the feature competitors hide)

For big money, "strategy available" is meaningless without "is it open, near capacity, or crowded?" Arrow Elite makes capacity a first-class, visible product surface:

| Control | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Capacity ledger | Per strategy by exchange/segment/symbol/expiry/time-bucket — in max lots, max notional, max participation rate, max concurrent accounts (not just AUM) |
| Per-client allocation cap | Each client's max capital/notional/lots in a strategy, set by liquidity, risk grade and current aggregate subscription |
| Capacity states (shown to client) | **Open · Limited · Waitlist · Closed-to-new-capital · Reduce-only · Paused · Retired** |
| Fill fairness | Deterministic allocation when many clients trigger the same order (pro-rata / time-sliced); no undisclosed priority |
| Decay monitoring | Track slippage-vs-model, reject rate, book-impact, hit ratio after costs, correlation with aggregate subscribed capital → pause/retire on breach |

## Risk, control and the client-owned-account promise

The single biggest trust differentiator: **the client keeps custody and control.** This is not PMS — there is no pooling, no discretionary mandate, no transfer of funds. The client can pause or kill any strategy and withdraw anytime.

| Layer | Control surface |
|---|---|
| Account | Total exposure, margin, cash/collateral split, withdrawal-safe cash, account-level kill switch |
| Strategy | Capital allocation, max drawdown halt, max slippage, order-reject ceiling, per-strategy kill switch |
| Instrument | Net Greeks, symbol/expiry concentration caps |
| Infra | Latency ledger, feed health, postback/sequence integrity, broker-controlled static egress |
| Human | Named market-hours desk, incident bridge, written RCA |

## Compliant quality display (no returns, ever)

The hard question every big-ticket prospect asks — *"what do these make?"* — cannot be answered with numbers. Here is what Arrow shows **instead**, and it is enough for a sophisticated diligence:

| Allowed | Banned |
|---|---|
| Strategy mechanism & category; instrument/segment scope | CAGR, monthly/expected return, backtested return headlines |
| Risk controls, capacity state, liquidity filters | "Top performer", star ratings, creator leaderboards by return |
| Operational metrics: uptime, reject rate, measured latency, halt counts | "HFT alpha for retail", profit screenshots as marketing |
| The client's **own** realized post-trade reporting (account statement, not marketing) | Implying expected profit in any pre-sale material |
| Backtest *methodology, assumptions, limitations* inside the RA dossier | Backtest *performance* in client-facing/marketing copy |

A **paper / live-shadow trial** lets a prospect watch a strategy operate on live data — with realistic slippage, rejects and MPP behaviour — before committing capital. That substitutes *demonstrated operation* for *promised returns*, which is both compliant and more convincing to a pro than any backtest chart.
