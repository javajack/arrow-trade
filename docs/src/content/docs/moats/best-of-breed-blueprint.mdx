This is the assembly manual: for every stage of the user journey, the broker that does it best today, exactly what to lift, and the *plus* that makes Arrow's version better rather than merely equal. The evidence for each cell is in the five experience-research pages; this page is the decision.

<Aside type="note" title="The integration thesis">
Read the table below and notice that **no broker appears in more than three rows**. Every incumbent wins a slice and is mediocre across the rest — which means today's serious user runs Zerodha for trust + Dhan for charts + Sensibull for strategies + m.Stock economics envy + Excel for journaling + Console for tax. The composite is not a feature list; it is the **removal of the seams** between best-in-class slices. That pipeline — chain → margin → cost → execution → journal → tax — is the product no one owns, and integrated pipelines are far harder to copy than any single slice.
</Aside>

## The five experience pillars

Rolling the table up, every lift lands in one of five pillars. These are the product's organizing principles — each pillar must be owned end-to-end by one team with one quality bar:

<CardGrid>
  <Card title="1 · Money moves like UPI" icon="seti:shell">
    In free and instant with visible limits; out instant including weekends; every rupee's state (settled, unsettled, collateral, withdrawable) always explained. Benchmark: beat Zerodha's published payout terms.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2 · Orders tell the truth" icon="approve-check">
    Predictable latency with per-order proof, exact reject reasons before and after submission, one audit trail across UI/chart/basket/API. Benchmark: nobody — this is white space.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3 · Cost is never a surprise" icon="seti:csv">
    All-in cost before, attributed statement after, funding interest in ₹/day, savings provable and shareable. Benchmark: m.Stock's price with Zerodha's clarity, then beyond both.
  </Card>
  <Card title="4 · Risk is visible before it bites" icon="warning">
    Margin previews, shortfall forecasts, rejected-leg simulation, expiry guardrails, delivery-risk countdowns, concentration analytics. Benchmark: Sensibull's analytics fused into execution.
  </Card>
  <Card title="5 · The platform remembers and explains" icon="open-book">
    Journal, strategy P&L, replay, contextual education, CA-ready reports, household view. Benchmark: Console's authority with INDmoney's breadth.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## What deliberately does *not* get lifted

Best-of-breed assembly fails when it copies indiscriminately. These proven-successful incumbent patterns are excluded on purpose:

| Pattern | Who runs it successfully | Why Arrow excludes it |
|---|---|---|
| Celebrity/IPL mass campaigns | Angel One, Upstox | High CAC, low-intent cohorts; contradicts proof-based brand. Upstox's decline (−27.6% FY26) shows awareness without product identity doesn't retain ([landscape](/market-landscape/)) |
| RM/telecalling-assisted conversion | Angel One, bank brokers | The wedge segments equate RMs with sales pressure; Dhan's "no RM" promise is a loved feature ([brand research](/experience/support-trust-brand/)) |
| Advisory/tips near execution | Angel, full-service brokers | Regulatory surface + trust contamination; research only with public scorecard, later |
| Voucher referral programs | Upstox (250+ brands) | Attracts coupon hunters; fee-offset credits attract traders |
| Insurance/loan cross-sell in-app | Upstox, ICICI | Trust contamination before broker credibility exists; Phase 3 at the earliest, opt-in |
| Teaser pricing with opaque recovery | Parts of the discount segment | One "why was I charged?" moment destroys more value than the teaser created |

## Sequencing the assembly

The full composite is years of work; the order is chosen so each layer generates the proof that markets the next:

1. **Launch (Phase 1):** pillars 1–3 complete + pillar 4's trading core (margin preview, true cost, chain, baskets, Latency Ledger v1) + free API with sandbox. This is the wedge promise: *money, orders and costs tell the truth.*
2. **Fast-follow:** Expiry Mode, journal + strategy P&L (pillar 5's trader half), paper trading, complaint dashboard, savings cards.
3. **Phase 2:** pillar 5's investor half — Portfolio Intelligence, MF/ETF/IPO/bonds, family workspace, research scorecard, contextual education.
4. **Phase 3:** ecosystem — partner OAuth, strategy lifecycle, pro-desk mode, Smart Cash, model portfolios.

Full launch/fast-follow/later assignments per feature are in the [Feature Parity Matrix](/strategy/feature-parity-matrix/) and the [Go-To-Market plan](/strategy/go-to-market/).

<Aside type="caution" title="The execution bar this implies">
A composite of best-in-class slices means being compared to the *best* incumbent in every slice simultaneously: payouts judged against Zerodha, charts against Dhan, price against m.Stock, calm against Groww. Shipping any slice at 80% of its benchmark reads as failure to that slice's natives. This is why the launch scope is narrow and the quality bar per pillar is absolute — better to launch with three pillars at benchmark-plus than five at benchmark-minus.
</Aside>
